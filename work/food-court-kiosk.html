Retail, self-service kiosk

Self-ordering kiosk for food courts

One kiosk takes orders for several small shops, and every shop needs its own receipt. This is how I designed the ordering flow so that every customer leaves with every receipt.

Role
End-to-end UX/UI design
Product
Self-ordering kiosk, sold to food brands
Setting
Food courts with juice, tea and meal counters

One kiosk, many counters

In a food court, the kiosk isn’t one restaurant’s till. It takes orders for several small shops at once: a juice counter, a tea stall, a meals counter, often standing side by side.

The kiosk is sold business to business, so the same product runs for different brands, each with its own shops and menus. The design had to work for all of them without being redrawn each time.

Where orders go missing

When an order covers three shops, the printer sends out three receipts, one after another. The first slip arrives quickly; the others take a few more seconds.

A screen that only says “thank you” doesn’t tell anyone that more slips are coming. It’s easy to take the first receipt and walk away, and a missing receipt means a customer at a counter with no proof of what they paid for.

Show every receipt as it prints

I reworked the post-payment screen around what is actually happening at the printer. The screen shows each receipt as it comes out, with a running count, so customers know how many slips to wait for and when they have them all.

  1. Make the printing visible

    The screen mirrors the printer. Each receipt appears on screen as it comes out, so it’s obvious there is more than one.

  2. Count every slip

    A running count, 1 of 3, 2 of 3, tells people how many to expect, and gives them a reason to stay for the last one.

  3. Say done only when it’s done

    The screen changes to its finished state when the count reaches the total, and then tells people where each receipt goes.

The whole order, end to end

I designed the full ordering experience, every screen from the first tap to the last receipt. This case study zooms in on the final screen because that’s where “one kiosk, many shops” is easiest to get wrong.

  1. Welcome
  2. Browse menus
  3. Your order
  4. Pay
  5. Collect receipts

The last screen isn’t a thank-you. Its job is to make sure every receipt leaves with its customer.
